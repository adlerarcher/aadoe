Planning and project tools

Twelve methods

Strategy analysis, performance framing, and delivery control. Use with Strategy Working Groups and Strategic Initiatives as appropriate. Entries are closed until selected.

01 · Strategy

Porter's Five Forces

Industry structure across five competitive forces.

Industry structure analysis across rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, threat of new entrants, and threat of substitutes. Used to judge competitive pressure and where strategy must defend or advance.

  • Map each force for the relevant market or mission area
  • Rate intensity and note structural drivers
  • Identify implications for positioning and partnerships
02 · Strategy

SWOT Analysis

Internal and external factors in four quadrants.

Organizes internal strengths and weaknesses against external opportunities and threats. Provides a shared baseline before choosing initiatives or portfolio adjustments.

  • Separate facts from judgments
  • Cross-match strengths to opportunities and weaknesses to threats
  • Convert findings into decision questions for leadership
03 · Strategy

PESTLE

External scan across six operating conditions.

Scans political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental conditions that shape the operating environment. Complements SWOT by focusing on external drivers.

  • List material factors under each letter
  • Note time horizon and certainty
  • Flag factors that require monitoring or scenario work
04 · Strategy

Value Chain Analysis

Primary and support activities that create or consume value.

Decomposes primary and support activities to locate where value is created, where cost accumulates, and where handoffs fail. Useful for process redesign and partnership design.

  • Map activities from input through delivery
  • Identify cost and quality drivers at each step
  • Target interventions where leverage is highest
05 · Performance

Balanced Scorecard

Strategy measures across four performance perspectives.

Links strategy to measures across financial, customer or stakeholder, internal process, and learning perspectives. Prevents single-metric management.

  • Select a small set of measures per perspective
  • Define targets and data sources
  • Review cause-and-effect links among perspectives
06 · Performance

OKRs and KPIs

Period objectives with key results and standing indicators.

Objectives and Key Results set directional aims with measurable outcomes. Key Performance Indicators track ongoing health. Used together to separate ambition from routine monitoring.

  • Write one clear objective for the period
  • Attach three to five measurable key results
  • Keep KPIs for standing operational signals
07 · Delivery

RACI Matrix

Role assignment for each activity and decision.

Assigns Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed roles for each activity. Clarifies ownership before work starts and reduces duplicate effort.

  • List decisions and deliverables as rows
  • Assign one Accountable owner per row
  • Limit Consulted and Informed to those who need the signal
08 · Delivery

Stakeholder Map

Parties plotted by influence and interest.

Plots parties by influence and interest to guide engagement cadence. Supports Strategy Working Groups that coordinate across offices and external partners.

  • Identify parties affected by or affecting the work
  • Place each on influence and interest axes
  • Set engagement mode for each quadrant
09 · Delivery

Risk Matrix

Likelihood and impact scoring for the register.

Rates risks by likelihood and impact so the register can prioritize response. Aligns with Tracking Register practice for status, risks, and dependencies.

  • Score likelihood and impact on a common scale
  • Plot items and set ownership for high cells
  • Record mitigation, contingency, and residual risk
10 · Delivery

WBS and Gantt

Scope packages placed on a timeline.

A Work Breakdown Structure decomposes scope into manageable packages. A Gantt chart places those packages on a timeline with dependencies and milestones.

  • Break scope into work packages with clear exits
  • Sequence packages and assign durations
  • Display milestones for leadership review
11 · Delivery

Critical Path Method

Longest dependent chain that sets duration.

Identifies the longest dependent chain of activities that determines project duration. Shows where delay is consequential and where float exists.

  • List activities, durations, and predecessors
  • Compute earliest and latest start and finish
  • Protect the critical path with active oversight
12 · Delivery

MoSCoW Prioritization

Must, Should, Could, and Will not for this cycle.

Sorts requirements into Must, Should, Could, and Will not for this cycle. Keeps scope negotiations explicit when capacity is fixed.

  • Define the delivery window and capacity constraint
  • Classify each requirement with one owner
  • Revisit classifications at each planning gate
Application

These methods support Organizational Development practice. Strategy Working Groups use them for framing. Initiative leads use them for delivery control. The Tracking Register records resulting status and decisions.
